Our Purpose

Why This Website Was Created

As a cybersecurity student interested in cybercrime and online safety, I wanted to create something that would help college students better understand their digital footprint and why it matters.

Most of us spend a lot of time online, whether we're using social media, shopping, applying for jobs, commenting on posts, or simply browsing the internet. All of these activities can leave behind information about us. Over time, those small pieces of information can add up and become part of our overall digital identity.

For college students, this is especially important. Many students are beginning to apply for internships, jobs, graduate programs, and other professional opportunities. What you post online today could potentially affect how someone views you in the future.

The Purpose of This Website

The goal of this website is to make digital footprints easier to understand. I want to provide college students with useful information about online reputation, privacy, cybersecurity, and the ways our online activity can follow us over time.

This website looks at both the information we choose to share and the information that can be collected about us while we use the internet. It also provides tips and resources for taking a more active role in managing your online presence.

Having a digital footprint doesn't mean you need to stop using social media or avoid being online. Instead, it's about being more aware of what you share, who can see it, and what information you may be leaving behind.

Why Your Digital Footprint Matters

Your online presence can play a role in how people perceive you. Employers, colleges, and professional organizations may search for information about applicants online. Public social media posts, photos, comments, and other content can sometimes give people an impression of who you are before they ever meet you.

A quote from cybersecurity professional Stéphane Nappo captures this idea well: "It takes 20 years to build a reputation but only a few minutes of a cyber incident to ruin it." While one post or photo doesn't necessarily define a person, it is still important to think about how the things we share could be viewed by others.

The purpose of this website isn't to tell students that everything they do online is bad or dangerous. It's about being aware of the potential consequences and making informed choices. Understanding your digital footprint gives you a better chance of protecting your reputation and presenting yourself in a positive way.

What You Will Learn

Throughout this website, you will learn about:

  • What a digital footprint is
  • The difference between active and passive digital footprints
  • How social media, photos, comments, and other online activity contribute to your digital identity
  • How your online presence can affect jobs, internships, and educational opportunities
  • Privacy and cybersecurity risks
  • Scams, phishing, and identity theft
  • Ways to check and improve your digital footprint
  • Resources and tools for managing your online presence

Your Digital Footprint Can Change

Your digital footprint may be difficult to completely erase, but that doesn't mean you're stuck with everything from your past.

The internet is a major part of modern life, and online presence is often unavoidable. What matters most is learning how to manage it effectively and responsibly.
