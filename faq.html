Resources for Managing Your Digital Footprint

Understanding your digital footprint is only the first step. The next step is figuring out what you can actually do about it. Managing your online presence does not mean deleting every social media account or trying to disappear from the internet. Instead, it means becoming more aware of what information is available about you and taking reasonable steps to protect your privacy, security, and reputation.

The resources below provide practical ways to check your online presence, protect your accounts, and learn more about online privacy and cybersecurity.

Check What People Can Find About You

Search Your Name

One of the simplest ways to start managing your digital footprint is to search for yourself online.

Try searching your:

  • Full name
  • Name and college
  • Name and hometown
  • Name and major
  • Common usernames
  • Email address

Look at the information that appears and think about what a professor, employer, graduate school, or professional organization might see if they searched for you.

Google also provides a feature called Results About You, which can help users find certain personal information, such as a phone number, home address, or email address, that appears in Google Search. In some situations, users can request that qualifying results be removed from Google Search. However, it is important to understand that removing a result from Google does not remove the information from the original website. (Google Search Help)

Resource: Google — Results About You

Review Your Social Media Accounts

Social media is one of the most visible parts of a person's digital footprint. Take some time to look through your current accounts and any older accounts you may have forgotten about.

Pay attention to:

  • Public posts
  • Photos and videos
  • Tagged photos
  • Comments
  • Public profile information
  • Old usernames
  • Information such as your phone number, address, or school schedule

You do not necessarily need to delete everything from your accounts. Instead, think about whether the content still represents you and whether you are comfortable with other people seeing it.

It is also important to remember that privacy settings do not give you complete control over what happens to something after you post it. Someone who can view your content may be able to save, screenshot, or share it.

Protect Your Online Accounts

Your digital footprint is connected to cybersecurity as well as your reputation. The accounts you use every day can contain personal information, private conversations, financial information, and other data that you do not want in the wrong hands.

One of the most important things you can do is use strong, unique passwords and enable multifactor authentication (MFA) when it is available.

MFA adds another step to the login process. Instead of relying only on a password, you may also have to verify your identity through an authentication app, security key, fingerprint, or another method. This provides an additional layer of protection if your password is compromised.

NIST also recommends using a password manager to create and store unique passwords. Its current guidance recommends that passwords used as a single authentication factor be at least 15 characters long.

Helpful Resources:

  • NIST — How Do I Create a Good Password?
  • NIST — Multi-Factor Authentication

Learn to Recognize Phishing

Phishing is another major cybersecurity concern connected to your digital footprint.

A phishing attack happens when someone tries to trick you into giving away information, clicking a malicious link, downloading a harmful file, or logging into a fake website. These messages may look like they came from a bank, college, employer, friend, or another organization you trust.

Phishing attempts can be difficult to recognize, especially because attackers can make messages look convincing. Before clicking a link or providing personal information, stop and consider:

  • Was I expecting this message?
  • Is the sender actually who they claim to be?
  • Is the message creating a sense of urgency?
  • Is it asking for my password, payment information, or verification code?
  • Does the link lead to a website I recognize?
  • Is there an attachment I was not expecting?

When in doubt, go directly to the organization's official website instead of clicking the link in the message.

Resource: CISA — Secure Our World

CISA recommends four basic steps for staying safer online: recognizing and reporting phishing, using strong passwords, turning on MFA, and keeping software updated.

Learn More About Online Privacy

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) provides information about online privacy, identity theft, scams, account security, and the ways websites and apps can collect and use personal information.

This is a useful resource if you want to understand not only what you post online, but also what information may be collected while you use websites and apps.

Resource: FTC — Online Privacy and Security

The FTC provides guides covering topics such as protecting personal information, securing accounts, recovering hacked social media accounts, and understanding how websites and apps collect information.

Build a Positive Online Presence

Managing your digital footprint is not only about removing information. You can also use the internet to build a positive professional presence.

For college students, this could include creating or updating a LinkedIn profile, building a portfolio, sharing academic or professional projects, or participating in conversations related to your field.

Social media can also be useful for career development. Pew Research Center found that social media has been used by job seekers to research jobs, find opportunities through their networks, and highlight professional accomplishments.

This is an important part of understanding your digital footprint: your online presence can create opportunities as well as risks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I completely erase my digital footprint?

Probably not.

Once information has been shared online, you may not be able to completely control where it goes. Someone may have copied, saved, or shared the information before you deleted it.

However, that does not mean you are stuck with everything you've ever posted. You can still remove information from accounts you control, adjust privacy settings, delete old accounts, and request removal of certain personal information when appropriate.

The goal is not to erase your entire digital footprint. The goal is to manage it responsibly.

Does having a digital footprint mean I should stop using social media?

No.

Social media can be a useful part of college life. It can help you stay connected with friends, participate in communities, learn about opportunities, and build professional relationships.

The important thing is to understand that what you share online can become part of your overall digital identity. Being thoughtful about what you post is more realistic than trying to avoid having an online presence altogether.

What should I do if I find something embarrassing about myself online?

First, don't panic.

If you control the account or website where the information was posted, see if you can remove it yourself. If someone else posted it, you can ask them to take it down.

If the information is appearing in Google Search and contains certain types of personal information, Google provides options for requesting removal of qualifying search results. Keep in mind that removing a result from Google Search does not remove the information from the original website.

Should I delete my old social media accounts?

If you no longer use an account, it may be worth considering whether you still need it.

Old accounts can contain information you may have forgotten about, including old photos, posts, usernames, and personal details. Removing accounts you no longer use can reduce the amount of information connected to your online identity.

Before deleting an account, make sure you save anything important that you want to keep.

Should my social media accounts be private?

There is no single answer that works for everyone.

Privacy settings can give you more control over who can see your content, but they cannot guarantee that information will never be shared. A person who can see your post may still be able to screenshot or share it.

A good approach is to review your privacy settings regularly and decide what information you are comfortable making public.

How often should I check my digital footprint?

There is no required schedule, but checking your online presence every few months is a reasonable habit.

It is especially useful to review your digital footprint before:

  • Applying for internships
  • Applying for jobs
  • Applying to graduate school
  • Attending a career fair
  • Starting a new job
  • Building a professional portfolio

A simple search of your name can help you understand what information is publicly available.

A Five-Minute Digital Footprint Check

If you are not sure where to begin, try this quick check:

  1. Search your name
  2. Check your social media
  3. Review your privacy settings
  4. Secure your accounts
  5. Think about your future

Ask yourself:

"Does my current online presence represent the person I want future employers, schools, and professional connections to see?"

You do not have to completely change your online life in one day. Start with one account, one privacy setting, or one old piece of information at a time.
