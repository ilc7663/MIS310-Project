01 / why it matters

What Is a Digital Footprint?

Think about everything you do online in a normal day. You might check Instagram, send a text or email, shop online, search for something, watch a video, fill out a form, or log into your bank account. Each of these activities can create information about you.

A digital footprint is the trail of data and information created by your online activities. Some of that information is something you intentionally share, while other information may be collected in the background as you use websites and apps.

Your digital footprint can include social media posts, photos, comments, emails, online purchases, account information, website visits, and other data connected to your online activity. It can also include information that other people post about you, such as a tagged photo or a comment mentioning your name.

The important thing to remember is that your digital footprint isn't just one thing. It's the collection of information that builds up over time as you interact with the digital world.

02 / two sides of your online presence

Active and Passive Digital Footprints

There are two common ways to think about a digital footprint: active and passive.

Active

Information you intentionally share online.

  • Social media posts
  • Comments
  • Online forms
  • Emails
  • Photos and videos

An active digital footprint is information you deliberately put online. Before posting, ask whether you would be comfortable with a professor, employer, or professional contact seeing it.

Passive

Information collected through your online activity.

  • Browsing history
  • Website visits
  • Cookies and trackers
  • Location data
  • Device information

A passive digital footprint is generated as you use websites and apps. Privacy settings, cookies, and app permissions help control what can be collected.

03 / key facts

Facts About Your Digital Footprint

  1. Your information can be difficult to completely remove

    If you delete a social media post, it may already have been saved, downloaded, or shared elsewhere.

  2. Your digital footprint can be traced

    Websites and online services can collect information about visits, devices, location, and interactions.

  3. Your online activity can create security risks

    Public personal details can make scams, phishing, and account attacks more convincing.

  4. Your activity can be used for advertising

    Browsing and shopping information may be used to personalize content and advertisements.

  5. Your digital footprint can contribute to identity theft risks

    Combined personal details can be used to impersonate someone or attempt fraud.

04 / your future

How Your Digital Footprint Can Affect Your Future

Your digital footprint isn't only a cybersecurity issue. It can also become part of your professional identity.

When you're in college, you're building more than a degree. You're also building a reputation, making professional connections, applying for internships, and preparing for your career.

Your online presence can become part of that process. A quick search of your name can help you understand what others might see.

Employment Opportunities

Employers may consider publicly available information when evaluating applicants. Search your name and review what appears.

Networking and Career Growth

A thoughtful LinkedIn profile, portfolio, or professional contribution can help show what you can do.

Your Personal Reputation

Friends, classmates, professors, organizations, and employers may see content that was meant for a smaller audience.

05 / manage it

How to Manage Your Digital Footprint

Search Your Name

Look at Google and your social media profiles to see what information is publicly available.

Review Your Social Media

Examine older posts, photos, comments, and tagged content to decide what you still want public.

Check Your Privacy Settings

Review account settings, app permissions, and cookies to limit unnecessary data collection.

Protect Your Accounts

Use strong, unique passwords and enable multifactor authentication whenever possible.

Think Before You Post

Ask whether you would be comfortable with the content being seen outside its original audience.

Build a Positive Online Presence

Add professional profiles, projects, volunteer work, and other information that reflects your goals.
